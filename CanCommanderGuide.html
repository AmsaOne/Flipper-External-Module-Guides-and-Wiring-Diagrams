1.2 Every Wire, One at a Time (10 Total)

Use this as a build checklist. The From and To columns use the exact silkscreen labels printed on each board — no jargon mixed in.

Reading the labels:
  • Flipper Zero / protoboard silkscreen: pin numbers (1-18) plus the GPIO name like PA7, PA6, PA4, PB3, PB2. There are no labels saying "MOSI" or "MISO" — those are SPI protocol roles, not printed on the board.
  • MCP2515 module silkscreen: VCC, GND, CS, SO, SI, SCK, INT (printed next to the 8-pin header).
  • OBD-II pigtail: pins 1-16, identified by wire color from the manufacturer's datasheet (varies — confirm with multimeter continuity if unsure).

1.4 OBD-II Pigtail — Which Pins You Actually Use

Your OBD-II pigtail is a male 16-pin connector with flying leads. Only three of the 16 wires are used:

        OBD-II Connector (looking at the face of the male plug)
         ┌──────────────────────────────────┐
         │  8   7   6●  5●  4●  3   2   1   │
         │                                  │
         │  16  15  14● 13  12  11  10  9   │
         └──────────────────────────────────┘

 Pin 4  ● Chassis Ground  →  Flipper Pin 8 / MCP2515 GND rail (common ground)
 Pin 5  ● Signal Ground   →  (same as pin 4 — use whichever your pigtail exposes;
                              if both, only one needs to be wired)
 Pin 6  ● CAN High        →  MCP2515 CANH (White)
 Pin 14 ● CAN Low         →  MCP2515 CANL (Brown)

 Pin 16  NOT USED (would be +12V — we're not tapping vehicle power)
Ignore and cap/trim all other pins. Don't let unused leads dangle where they can short.

1.5 Ground Rule (Read This — Still Important)

Even though we're not using vehicle 12V, we still need a common ground reference between the Flipper and the vehicle. The CAN transceiver's differential signals are referenced to vehicle ground; the Flipper's SPI is referenced to its own ground. Without a bond between them, the MCP2515's CAN-H/CAN-L readings can float out of the transceiver's valid input range and produce garbage data.

 Flipper Pin 8 ──────┬─────── MCP2515 GND ──────┬───────── OBD Pin 4 or 5
                     │                           │
                     └───── one ground net ──────┘

Three points, one net. That's the rule.

2. Assembly Order

  1. Solder the protoboard headers if not already done — female headers on the side facing away from the Flipper body so F-F jumpers plug in easily
  2. Wire the power pair (#1 Red to VCC, #2 Black to GND)
  3. Wire the SPI bundle (#3-6) — keep these short and bundled together
  4. Wire the CAN pair from the OBD pigtail (#7 CANH, #8 CANL)
  5. Wire the ground bond from OBD to Flipper (#9) — tie into the same black rail as #2
  6. Cap or trim every unused wire on the OBD pigtail
  7. Enable Flipper 5V: Settings → GPIO → 5V on GPIO Pin → ON
  8. Launch the Electronic Cats MCP2515 app; verify module detection (no vehicle needed for this step — SPI enumeration works without the bus connected)

3. Flipper App

App: Electronic Cats flipper-MCP2515-CANBUS

Install:

  1. Download the .fap from https://github.com/ElectronicCats/flipper-MCP2515-CANBUS/releases
  2. Copy to Flipper SD card: /apps/GPIO/mcp2515.fap (use qFlipper or an SD card reader)
  3. On the Flipper: Apps → GPIO → MCP2515 CAN Bus
  4. First screen should show module detected. If not, go back to section 1 and verify wires #1-6.

5. Safety

  • Never plug into a moving vehicle's OBD port. Car parked, engine can be on or off.
  • Never clear DTCs before an emissions inspection.
  • Frame injection (writing to the bus) can affect ABS, steering, airbags. For monitoring-only the Electronic Cats app defaults to read-only.
  • Flipper GPIO pins 10 and 12 are SWD debug (silkscreen SWC / SIO). Do not connect anything to them, ever.
  • Always bond OBD ground to Flipper ground (wire #10). A floating ground with a live CAN bus can damage the transceiver.
